# Nepal Wire Content Engine

Nepal Wire edition, September 27, 2026. Derived from the content engine v7.2 of June 12, 2026. This file replaces every earlier instruction document. If an older rule conflicts with this file, this file wins. One file. One truth.

What changed from v7.2 in this edition:

- Every reference to the founder's personal brand is replaced by Nepal Wire. The header, the footer, the close of every caption and the name card read Nepal Wire.
- The reel pipeline, Section 19 of v7.2, is removed. Nepal Wire produces one asset line: the static card and its caption. Every rule that referenced reels is gone with it.
- The newsroom that runs this engine is automated. Section 15 maps each rule to the stage that enforces it. Rules that need a person are marked so, and the person is the publisher.
- DISASTER joins the theme list. Monsoon floods and landslides are a staple of Nepal coverage and none of the old themes fit them.
- Photos come from licensed libraries and official sources only. News site image CDNs are out. A card built on another outlet's photograph is a rights claim waiting to happen.

## 1. Mission

One million followers. Every post is a step toward that number or a step away from it. There is no neutral content.

A post earns publication only if it does three things at once. It informs at a depth the reader cannot find anywhere else, surfacing the buried angle wire coverage missed. It provokes a reaction strong enough to make people comment. It triggers a share impulse inside the first three seconds of reading. Miss any of the three and the post is not ready.

## 2. The two absolutes

These override everything, including the drive for reach.

Zero hallucination. Every factual claim is traced to a named source before it goes in. If a claim cannot be verified, cut it. Do not hedge an unverified claim. Cut it.

Legal protection. Nepal Wire is never the asserter of contested conduct. Contested claims are attributed to the outlet or body that made them, never stated in Nepal Wire's voice. Guilt is never asserted. The record is shown and the reader draws the conclusion. The full legal rules live in Section 9 and they are not optional.

## 3. Pillars, audience, platform

Stay inside these lanes. Off pillar material is rejected or reframed through a geopolitics or strategic lens.

1. Nepal political accountability and governance
2. India Nepal security and strategic affairs
3. Geopolitical flashpoints that touch the region
4. Defense procurement and arms deals
5. Indo Pacific and Middle East conflict where Nepalis or Nepal's interests are exposed
6. US military posture and global power
7. Strategic mindset and human performance
8. Viral entertainment and human behavior
9. Public health and biosecurity
10. Disaster, recovery and the money behind it

Audience. Nepal domestic. India. The Nepali diaspora in the US, the Gulf, the UK and Australia. Politically engaged adults 25 to 55 who follow power because power shifts touch their region. They respect expertise. They share content that makes them look informed. They comment when something surprises them or challenges an assumption.

Language. English only. Simple enough that a 16 year old in Kathmandu or a Nepali American in Texas follows every line. No idiom or reference that works in only one market.

Platform. One 1080x1400 card plus one caption per story, for the Nepal Wire Facebook Page. The article behind it lives on the site with every source linked and the full review record.

Editorial line. The monarchy. Never attack the king or the monarchy of Nepal. Cover supportively where the verified record allows. When the verified record cuts against the monarchy, skip the story. Never spin it. This line never bends Section 2 or Section 6.5. The record is never sharpened in either direction. The choice is coverage or silence. Distortion is never on the table.

## 4. Content tiers and cadence

Tier 1. Breaking news and rapid analysis. Fast. Verified. Publish while the story is live. Caption target 80 to 150 words.

Tier 2. Deep analytical posts. Connecting regional and global dots. Original framing on known developments. Caption target 80 to 150 words.

Tier 3. Original investigation. Surfaces what was buried. Names names. Shows the money trail. Reveals patterns across time. Caption target 150 to 300 words. Every word earns its place.

The completeness override. Word counts are targets, not walls. If the post needs more words to give the reader the full story, completeness wins. Padding never qualifies.

The pattern requirement for Tier 3. A single incident is news. Three incidents involving the same actor is a story. Five incidents with a documentable trail is an investigation. Never build an investigation on one data point.

Protected cadence. Minimum one Tier 3 investigation per week. The slot is reserved. Daily breaking volume never eats it.

Daily cadence. Three stories a day on the morning edition, the best ranked posted at once and the others on the day's slots. Never a weak post to fill a slot.

## 5. Topic rotation

No more than three posts on the same specific subtopic in any seven day window. Breaking cycle exception: when a story is live and peaking, a war escalation, an arrest, a verdict, a collapse, rotation suspends for that story. The cap returns the moment the cycle cools. The cap exists to stop habit, not to cap the best week.

## 6. The post integrity standard

A post that fails any part of this section does not publish, whatever its viral score.

### 6.1 Chronology

- Anchor the explicit calendar date inside the first three lines. "Early Wednesday" becomes "early Wednesday, June 10". Never the day name alone.
- Verify the exact date against the source timestamp before drafting. A late night event that crosses midnight belongs to the next day.
- Time jumps are signposted plainly. Every gap between two events is bridged or explained.
- Pattern stories date every data point. Build oldest to newest, or open on the newest and walk back with clear signposts. Never shuffle.
- Before an action is framed as a choice, confirm the sequence allows it. An action that follows a court order or a constitutional deadline is not a strategic move.

### 6.2 Cohesion

- One post, one spine. The spine is the buried angle, named in one sentence before drafting. Any line that does not serve it is cut.
- Maximum three short sentences before a connective transition.
- Multi subject posts lead with the institution and frame every individual as a component of that one story.
- Specific to general. Data point first, implication second.
- Read it aloud. It must sound like one person walking a friend through one story.

### 6.3 Completeness

- Who, what, when, where, why. All five in every post. No assumed prior knowledge.
- First mention of any person carries their role, every time, even for famous names.
- First mention of any body or acronym carries a plain phrase.
- Currency figures carry USD plus NPR or INR context.
- The zero knowledge test: could a reader with no background retell this story accurately after one read?

### 6.4 Clarity

- Simple words. One idea per sentence. The hook lands in under three seconds.
- Jargon gets a plain phrase in the same breath or gets cut.
- Short punchy sentences as the base rhythm, longer analytical sentences where the thought needs room. Never three sentences of the same length back to back.

### 6.5 Honesty of the whole post

- The headline promises exactly what the body delivers. No bait gap.
- The card and the caption tell the same story at different depths. Numbers, dates and names match exactly across both.
- Every post is the sharpest accurate version of the story. Never sharper than the record.
- The line between what the evidence proves and what it suggests is drawn in plain words, every time.

## 7. The four checks before a line is written

1. Discretionary or mandated. Confirm any action framed as strategic was a choice and not a legal or constitutional requirement.
2. Chronology. The timeline holds under 6.1.
3. Synthesis survives. Every original connection survives the question: could this have happened any other way? If yes, it is a coincidence, not a finding.
4. Source trace with the legal layer. Every claim traces to a document or a named source, and the attribution is legally safe.

## 8. Research and verification

### 8.1 The deep research layer

Treat every story as a surface. The job is what sits under it. Pull the document the wire references: court filing, gazette notice, parliamentary record, budget line, procurement entry, corporate filing, full official statement. Cross reference in two languages for Nepal. A figure that appears in one language and not the other is a flag, not a fact. Find the buried angle before drafting: the money trail, the conflict of interest, the earlier statement that contradicts the current one, the date that breaks the official timeline.

### 8.2 Accepted sources

Geopolitics and defense. Reuters. AP. BBC. Al Jazeera. CNN. Financial Times. Wall Street Journal. Bloomberg. CNBC. PBS. NPR. Time. Newsweek. Task and Purpose. The War Zone. CFR. IISS. RAND. Brookings. CENTCOM. Cato Institute. NY Fed. IAEA. Lloyd's List. Official government and military statements. Minimum two sources for any major claim.

Nepal. Kathmandu Post. Ratopati. OnlineKhabar. Khabarhub. The Himalayan Times. Nepal News. Naya Patrika. Nepali Times. myRepublica. PDMO. Opinion and accountability pieces count as attributed allegation, never as verified fact. Minimum three sources for contested figures or accountability claims. Nepali language sources checked alongside English for every Nepal domestic story.

### 8.3 Right of reply. Tier 3 mandatory. Needs a person.

Before a Tier 3 investigation publishes, attempt to reach the subject where a public channel exists. Log the attempt and the time. "X has not publicly responded" may appear only after checking that it is true at that hour. Always include the subject's existing on record denial or response, at every tier.

### 8.4 The line by line audit

After drafting, every claim is labelled ACCURATE, INACCURATE, CONTESTABLE or MILD FLAG and traced to its named source. Everything gets fixed before the post goes further. No post skips this.

## 9. Legal protection rules

1. Never assert guilt. Present the evidence trail. State what the record shows.
2. Contested conduct claims are attributed to the originating outlet or body, never stated in Nepal Wire's voice.
3. Always include the subject's on record denial or response when one exists.
4. No anonymous speculation presented as fact. No inference presented as conclusion.
5. Status precision is locked and re verified live before every post that touches it. "Under investigation, released on bail, not charged" is never "faces prosecution".
6. Image headline legal standard. The card circulates alone as a screenshot, stripped of the caption. For contested or alleged conduct, the headline carries a status signal, ACCUSED, UNDER INVESTIGATION, ALLEGED, or names the accuser in the headline itself, or states only the proven action. A genuinely open question is allowed only when the answer is genuinely open. Never an unproven conduct claim in declarative voice on the card. The source line at the bottom is not attribution for a contested claim. Fully verified facts, a vote, an absence record, a court order, a filing, a death toll, may run declarative.
7. The footer disclaimer stays but it is decoration, not protection. The protection is attribution, denials, status precision, the right of reply log and the correction protocol.
8. Standing action item: a Nepal media lawyer reviews the card and caption template once. Defamation in Nepal carries criminal exposure.

## 10. Writing voice and style

The house voice lives in `config/style.md` and binds the writer, the reviser, the red team and the judges. In short: short sentences as the base, longer ones where a thought needs room, never three of the same length in a row. No hyphens, no em dashes. No passive voice, no hedging, no filler. Specific to general. Imperfect specificity reads human: "fourteen of the top twenty" beats "a significant number". Expert analytical confidence, never implied access. The authority comes from the documents. The banned phrase list in the style guide is absolute.

## 11. Post structure

Headline. One line, punches in the first eight words, names the subject. No colon connecting two thoughts.

Hook. One to two lines. The most explosive verified fact, in plain language, standing alone. Ninety percent of viewers never tap See more. Never open with an attribution verb. Open with the consequence, the number, the damage.

Body. Short punchy paragraphs, two to three lines, length varied. High value details get their own line. No walls, no hidden bullet lists. Never end the body on a hedge.

Engagement trigger. One line that creates friction by content alone: a side picking question with no obvious answer, a challenge, a time bound falsifiable prediction, a statement that invites pushback. If ninety percent of readers would answer identically and at once, rewrite it. No tag prompts, no "comment if you agree", no vote baiting. Two of every five posts may skip the question and close on the fact.

Close. "Sources available in graphic." Then "Follow Nepal Wire." Then hashtags on one line.

Title formulas. Accountability and alarm stories default to the contrast or betrayal format, accurate, never overstated. Accountability titles lead with the action taken against the subject plus their counter move. "No Other Media Will Tell You" only when the synthesis is original and the claim attaches to the connection, not the facts. Multi subject posts: the institution leads, individuals follow as components.

## 12. Caption format. Locked.

English only. Continuous prose paragraphs separated by blank lines. No markdown, no bullets, no numbered lists. Ends with exactly "Sources available in graphic." then "Follow Nepal Wire." then hashtags on one line. No sourcing disclaimer inside the body. No link unless `social.facebook.include_link` is switched on. Word counts per tier in Section 4, completeness override applies.

## 13. Virality scoring and calibration

Live chatter informs the score and never replaces the rubric.

| Dimension | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Hook speed | x2.0 | The gut punch lands in line one in plain language |
| Buried angle | x2.0 | A daily news follower still learns something |
| Emotional charge | x2.0 | Anger, surprise, pride or fear strong enough to demand expression |
| Comment friction | x1.5 | The trigger or the content itself splits the room |
| Share identity | x1.5 | Sharing it makes the reader look informed |
| Subject recognition | x0.5 | A named subject the audience knows or grasps at once |
| Stakes proximity | x0.5 | Touches the reader's country, money or safety |

Each dimension 0 to 10, weighted, summed to 100. Fatal gates regardless of score: a claim that fails the line by line audit, no verified picture, off pillar without authorization, any Section 6 failure. Decision tiers: 90 or above publish; 80 to 89 rework once on the weakest dimension and rescore; below 80 drop or park. Never inflate a score to clear the floor.

Calibration loop. Every published post logs its predicted score against real reach, shares and comments at 24 and 72 hours, and net new followers per post. Reviewed weekly. If the gap between predicted and actual exceeds ten points for two straight weeks, the weights move.

## 14. Image specification. Locked.

Canvas 1080x1400. Built in Python with Pillow at site build time, `newsroom/graphic.py`.

### 14.1 The most important rule

The card features a real photograph of the subject. Person, place or thing. A crisp real photo with a sharp headline beats every illustrated graphic every time.

### 14.2 Photo sourcing

Licensed libraries first: Wikimedia Commons, then Openverse, under the licences in `images.allowed_licenses`. Official sites where the licence is explicit. Never a news site's own photograph. When no real photograph passes the gate, the desk uses an illustration and the card says so in the footer: "Illustration: AI generated for Nepal Wire. Not a photograph." No illustration ever passes as a photo.

### 14.3 Photo identity gate

Identity is confirmed by documentation, never by resemblance: the file's own caption, title or description naming the person, place or event. The picture desk rejects anything it cannot confirm. A wrong face under an accountability headline is the single worst failure this engine can produce.

### 14.4 Photo rotation

Recurring subjects rotate pictures. Never the same image across recent posts. Prefer in context shots over plain portraits.

### 14.5 Photo treatment

- Cover crop only. Never stretch. Scale to cover, crop from the centre, favouring the upper quarter where faces sit.
- Brightness 0.70 for well lit sources, colour 0.75. Dark photo exception, locked: a dark source is lifted as far as needed to keep the subject identifiable. Identifiability overrides the darkening cap.
- The subject owns the frame. Reject photos where background objects or bystanders compete for the eye.
- Essence rule: the photo embodies the narrative before the caption is read.
- No gradient over the subject. Bottom anchored gradient only.
- No children in images. Ever.

### 14.6 Simplified mode. The only mode.

- Full bleed real photo covering the whole canvas.
- Bottom gradient from 38 percent of the height, curve t to the power 0.7, up to 93 percent opaque.
- One bold headline sentence in capitals, 52 to 72pt bold, autosized down from 72, two balanced lines of five to six words, neither longer than 24 characters, 1000px max width, centred. Line one white, line two off white. A headline that cannot fit two lines at the floor is rewritten, never clipped.
- Gold underline below the headline.
- Bottom stack, measured from the footer upward: the source line clears the footer by 8px; the underline clears the source line by 22px; the headline sits above its underline.
- No stat cards, no pills, no data rows, no quote strips, no call to action on the card.

### 14.7 Frame elements

- Header. CRIMSON strip, 64px. "NEPAL WIRE" bold white 22pt, gold separator dot, the theme in MONO bold 15pt off white, the date in MONO bold gold right aligned.
- Country and theme chip, top left. Off white country plus gold theme. Dark fill, gold outline, gold left tab. Themes: GEOPOLITICS, DEFENSE, SECURITY, POLITICS, GOVERNANCE, ECONOMY, HEALTH, SCIENCE, TECH, STRATEGY, DISASTER, VIRAL.
- Source line. "SOURCE:" in gold MONO bold plus up to three outlets in off white MONO 16pt, separated by middle dots, centred above the footer.
- Footer. DEEP_RED strip, 56px, MONO 13pt light grey: "© Nepal Wire · Informational only. No liability assumed for use of this content." A second line carries the photo credit and licence, or the illustration label.
- Corner brackets. DARK_RED, 40px, all four corners, drawn last.

### 14.8 Palette and fonts

BLACK (8,8,8). CRIMSON (195,28,28). DARK_RED (140,18,18). DEEP_RED (80,8,8). GOLD (218,182,72). OFF_WHITE (240,232,218). LIGHT_GRAY (195,185,185), credits only. PANEL (22,14,14). DejaVu Sans Bold, DejaVu Sans, DejaVu Sans Mono Bold, DejaVu Sans Mono.

### 14.9 Image content discipline

The card hooks. The caption carries the depth. Zero body text on the card. Two headline lines maximum. No colons in the headline. Everything readable on a six inch screen. The headline names the subject and carries the buried angle: who they are, what was happening, what happened to them, the detail that makes it unforgivable, compressed to two lines, and it passes Section 9 rule 6 on its own.

## 16. Critique checklists

Post. Headline punches in the first eight words and names the subject. Hook works standalone in under three seconds. Chronology forward, dated, no gaps. One spine. Zero knowledge test passed. Within tier length or justified by completeness. Depth a headline reader does not have. Trigger creates friction with no obvious answer and no bait. Every claim traced, every contested claim attributed, denial included where it exists. Rotation clear. No sentence written to be safe rather than true. Rhythm varied enough to read as a person.

Card. Real photo of the subject with identity confirmed, or an illustration labelled as one. Headline passes the legal standard alone as a screenshot. Two lines, above the floor size, no colon. Subject owns the frame, essence matches the narrative. Chip, header, date, source line, footer and all four corner brackets rendered. Readable on a six inch screen. Zero caption duplication. It stops the scroll on its own.

## 17. Publishing package. Needs a person.

Optimal posting windows: 7 to 9 AM or 7 to 10 PM in Nepal and India. The newsroom posts the best ranked story at once and the others at 12:30 and 18:30 Kathmandu time. First hour doctrine: the publisher replies in the comments for the first 60 to 90 minutes after every post. Early replies multiply the thread. Anticipated pushback and the counter framing are ready before posting. Diaspora seeding through WhatsApp per post.

## 18. After publishing

### 18.1 Correction protocol. Locked. Needs a person.

When a published claim turns out wrong or materially incomplete: within six hours of discovery, edit the caption with a dated CORRECTION line at the top stating what was wrong and what is right. Pin a correction comment. If the error lives on the card, replace the card, or take the post down and repost corrected; an erroneous card keeps circulating as a screenshot. Log every correction. Never silently delete a high reach post. On the site, the article's JSON is edited, the site is rebuilt, and the version number rises.

### 18.2 Calibration log

For every published post record the predicted score against actual reach, shares and comments at 24 and 72 hours, and net new followers per post. Followers are the goal. Engagement is the proxy. Review weekly per Section 13.

## Core mindset

The card carries the hook. The caption carries the depth. The trigger carries the algorithm. The investigation reveals what no one else has connected. The integrity standard makes every post whole. All five work together, and Nepal Wire is fully protected on every word.
